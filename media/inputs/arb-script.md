## Scene 3: Beat 2.1: Cross-Exchange Arbitrage

narration: Cross-exchange arbitrage is the textbook version: buy where it's cheap, sell where it's expensive. The trap beginners fall into is buying on Exchange A, hitting withdraw, and waiting ten minutes while the price gap vanishes before the coins even land. People who actually run this hold inventory on both sides ahead of time, fire both orders at once, and rebalance balances later when things are quiet.

## Scene 4: Beat 2.2: Triangular Arbitrage

narration: What if you don't even cross exchanges? Triangular arbitrage looks for pricing mismatches across three pairs on the same platform, like trading stablecoins for Bitcoin, Bitcoin for another coin, and back to stablecoins. If the implied rates loop back slightly higher than where you started after fees, you pocket the spread. Sounds neat on paper, until leg two fills slower than leg one and leaves you holding a bag you didn't plan on.

## Scene 5: Beat 2.3: Funding-Rate Arbitrage

narration: Then there's funding-rate arbitrage, basically the crypto version of cash-and-carry. When perpetual futures trade above spot and funding is positive, traders shorting perps get paid periodically. Buy spot, open an equal short on the perp, and price swings mostly cancel out while you collect funding, as long as funding doesn't flip negative or your margin gets squeezed.
callout: FUNDING
direction: Calm, measured medium shot, split screen with a balance meter. SPOT LONG box on the left, PERP SHORT box on the right, prices tick while the net stays near zero and a FUNDING PAYOUT ticker drips in each interval, then the boxes slide off to the right.

## Scene 6: Beat 3.1: Fees, Slippage, and Thin Order Books

narration: Here's the part people skip when they screenshot a price gap: top-of-book prices lie to larger orders. If there's only a tiny amount of liquidity sitting at the best price, your order walks up the book and fills worse. That's slippage. Add two trading fees, network costs, and spread friction, and a 'great' gap often turns flat or negative.
callout: SLIPPAGE
direction: Medium close-up on the host with a deadpan sigh, cut to a stacked bar graphic. A green GROSS SPREAD bar enters, then red TAKER FEES x2 and SLIPPAGE blocks that grow with order size and eat the green bar until the net turns red.

## Scene 7: Beat 3.2: Leg Risk, Stale Data, and Exchange Friction

narration: The nastiest bug in arbitrage isn't bad math. It's a half-finished trade. Your buy fills, your sell gets rejected or slips past your limit, and suddenly your 'market-neutral' arbitrage is just a regular directional bet in a volatile market. Throw in stale market data, rate limits, paused withdrawals, or exchange risk, and 'easy money' turns into babysitting errors. That's why automated systems with real-time feeds and strict risk rules dominate the fast gaps.

## Scene 8: Beat 4.1: What Actually Matters Before You Touch a Dollar

narration: If you want to understand arbitrage without getting cooked, start with the math after every cost, not the raw price gap. Check real order book depth, watch how fast a spread actually sticks around, and assume something will eventually lag or fail. Arbitrage isn't a cheat code. It's execution discipline under tight margins.
